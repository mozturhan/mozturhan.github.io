About me

I have a Ph.D. in Linguistics at the University of Kansas. I use psycholinguistic and neurolinguistic approaches to investigate language comprehension across the lifespan, focusing on adult and child first language (L1) speakers and adult second language (L2) learners, in order to understand whether successful sentence comprehension relies on qualitatively similar or different processing mechanisms across these groups, and to identify the factors that facili-tate language comprehension. To address these questions, I have developed expertise in a range of behavioral, psycholinguistic, and neurolinguistic methods, including electroencephalography (EEG), eye-tracking, self-paced reading, and judgment tasks. Applying these methods across adult L1 and L2 populations allows me to investigate whether successful comprehension relies on qualitatively similar or different processing mechanisms across groups, and to identify the factors that facilitate language comprehension.

Current focus

  • Predictive processing in Turkish — The temporal dynamics of argument–verb computations in Turkish: Comprehenders rapidly restrict verb prediction to structural arguments, but argument role information impacts prediction only when additional time is available before the verb. More →
  • Subject-verb agreement in L2 Turkish — How L1 features and individual variation in proficiency level impacts the acquisition of animacy-conditioned agreement in Turkish (under review, Linguistic Approaches to Bilingualism). More →
  • Children's comprehension of 'hard' words — How discourse organization through focus markign and Question Under Discussion helps preschoolers interpret universal quantifiers in Turkish and English (under review, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience). More →
